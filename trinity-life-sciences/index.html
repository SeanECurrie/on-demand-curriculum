▸ The honest version of what the system does and doesn't do

The system amplifies expertise. It does not fabricate it. What the system did NOT do

  • Did not trust training data. Every Trinity finding is anchored to a 2025-2026 source. The Parthenon-vs-Kohlberg PE-sponsor question was actively resolved against current sources, not assumed from older information.
  • Did not pick one number when sources disagreed. Trinity self-reports 1,300 employees; LinkedIn shows 1,672; Inc. 5000 cites "over 1,200" (Aug 2024). All three surfaced, reconciliation explained, ranges treated as directional.

  • Did not pretend the operator wrote every line by hand. The system did the heavy lifting on research and structural construction. The operator did the calibration work — which findings to lead with, which contradictions to surface, what the synthesis should land on.
  • Did not surface insider knowledge of TGaS or Trinity. Public-source-only discipline was locked into every research thread. The deliverable adds nothing Brian doesn't already know about Trinity's internal workings; it adds the calibrated external read he can layer his insider knowledge on top of.

Honest caveats — what this can't do well

  • Domains with no public surface. If the target company has no LinkedIn presence, no press releases, no analyst coverage, and no third-party citations — the system will produce a thin briefing with a lot of "absence is signal" framing. Worse than useless if the reader expects fact density.
  • Audience definition is load-bearing. This briefing was sharp because the audience profile was specific: "Brian, Head of CMIT, weighing cut-bait-or-stay on Trinity LS, reading with Jimmy in the loop." Generic-audience briefings produce generic-quality briefings. The intake matters.
