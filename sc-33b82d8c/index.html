⚙How the plan was derived — the full thought process

Every allocation decision, with the tradeoffs shown. This is the part you can attack.

  1. The constraint set: $700 committed (not advisory — spend it well, don't re-litigate it) · 2-3 weeks · ~6 hrs/day = 84-126 total hours · goals in your words: new resume-claimable names, confidence selling yourself, invigoration and knowledge. Free courses allowed on top.
  2. The window math forced a one-big-exam rule. CAPM (feeder + prep ≈ 55-80h) and Salesforce Admin (org build + prep ≈ 50-80h) each fit the window alone; both together = 105-160h — over budget unless everything goes perfectly. Promising two exams in two weeks is how plans fail. So: one exam inside the window, the second staged immediately after with money already committed, so it can't silently die.
  3. Why Salesforce got the in-window slot over CAPM: four reasons. (a) Your joy criterion — mastery through building; the Dev-org build is construction, CAPM is memorization. (b) The org build produces an artifact CAPM can't. (c) "Salesforce Certified" ties CAPM on recognition but ALSO resolves the ecosystem-lane open question by simply existing. (d) The build doubles as exam prep — no wasted hours. CAPM isn't demoted, it's sequenced: its feeder starts week 3, exam ~week 5, dollars already allocated.
  4. Why the blitz week comes first, not the big exam: momentum economics. Four credential names + a live artifact inside five days converts enthusiasm into evidence immediately (your invigoration goal), de-risks the plan (if week 2 slips, week 1's wins already happened), and the blitz items have hard external dependencies (Gainsight env lead time, CCEP price check) that need firing early anyway.
  5. The Day-1 slow-fuse principle: every item with lead time or scheduling friction (Gainsight env email, sandbox/org creation, the exam booking) fires before any coursework. The booked SF exam date is a deliberate commitment device — an unbooked exam is a wish.
  6. The $100 retake line is insurance, not pessimism: the SF exam was revised Dec 2025 (prep material can be stale against the new outline); $100 reserved means a bad day costs a week, not the plan. Pass clean and it rolls to Tableau DS — after its artifact, per brief C's artifact-first verdict.
  7. PMI membership math on CAPM: join ($99ish) + member exam ($225) ≈ non-member $300 but adds retake discount + PDU resources — roughly cost-neutral with better downside. The ~$280 line reflects it.
  8. What was rejected for this window and why: CCMP — $1.6K wouldn't fit $700 anyway, and the recognition crack (§4) is unresolved · WalkMe/SAP exam ($230-276) — would fit, but hands-on access is unverified, which fails your 30-hour doctrine; a cert without loggable hours is exactly what you said you won't stand on · ServiceNow — 150-250h doesn't fit any 2-week window and the experience gate stands · everything in §6.
  9. Ledger discipline on timing: nothing goes on the resume until its credential/artifact exists — which in this plan means most additions land within days of each other in week 1-2, not at some far horizon. The plan IS the claim-sequencing your doctrine demands, compressed.

◎Everything considered — the full research landscape

The shelf (§5) shows the finalists. This is the whole universe each brief actually evaluated and read through — including everything that fell out before becoming a card, what got read to decide, and each brief's own strongest argument AGAINST its own pick. ~40 credentials/paths evaluated across 7 briefs; 20 reached the shelf; 8 reached the play.

Brief A — Project management · "Which PM credential changes hiring outcomes for CS/onboarding/implementation roles — and does any beat no-credential-plus-practice-evidence?"

Evaluated (7): CAPM · Google PM Certificate · PSM-I · CSM (Scrum Alliance — same signal as PSM-I at 2-3x price + $100/2yr renewal treadmill) · CompTIA Project+ (~$358 — IT/gov recognition channel, 0 corpus mentions, lost its good-for-life status Oct 2025) · PRINCE2 Foundation ($600-750 — UK public-sector credential, US recognition minimal, UK vacancies down 62% since 2023) · PMP as strategic backdrop (not a candidate now: requires 36 documented months — but your 15 yrs kitchen leadership + 3 yrs SaaS onboarding is plausibly documentable later; CAPM doesn't feed PMP eligibility but the study substrate overlaps).

What got read: PMI official docs, r/PMCareers + r/projectmanagement + r/scrum + r/pmp threads (2024-2026, upvote-weighted), Scrum Alliance/scrum.org official terms, UK postings-market analysis, and three live implementation JDs printing "PMP or CAPM" verbatim. Key texture: a career-changer passed CAPM in ~a week of memorization after the Google cert — cheap to get, and HMs know it's knowledge-only; "certs open doors, don't award jobs" recurred across every thread.

The brief's own counter-argument: your funnel says the surface CAPM improves (cold ATS) is the surface that doesn't convert for you; one CS hiring manager was quoted saying "PMs have no place in CS" to a CSM holding a PMP (countered in-thread). Forced to one play, the funnel evidence favors practice-proof over paper — the rebuttal being that 16/55 REQUIRED-flag JDs make the skills-line gap real and the plays aren't exclusive.

Brief B — Salesforce + HubSpot · "Does the Admin cert change outcomes for non-admin roles — or do employers want experience the cert doesn't provide?"

Evaluated (6): SF Platform Administrator cert (via a three-class analysis: companies that USE Salesforce — cert doesn't move screening, experience-language dominates, even Salesforce's own CSM postings ask for hands-on not certs · Salesforce-ECOSYSTEM partners/ISVs — cert genuinely gates, sometimes required · actual admin roles — saturated market, "cert is the floor not the differentiator") · SF Associate ($75 — rejected: ecosystem insiders discount it themselves: "I don't put too much value on the Associate") · free Dev-org build (the actual experience-gap closer) · HubSpot Academy certs · Attio (confirmed: no cert program exists) · vendor-neutral "CRM administration" credential (searched: none exists with market weight).

What got read: help.salesforce.com + Trailhead official pricing/exam docs (post-Dec-2025 revision), Salesforce Ben + Apex Hours trade press, an ex-Salesforce Principal CSM and a CS hiring manager on r/CustomerSuccess ("the worst CSM I've worked with out of close to 1000 had multiple certs"), Ask a Manager practitioner threads, HubSpot's own community ("nice-to-have, not must-have").

The brief's own counter-argument: hire-fast screens are keyword-binary and silent rejections are unmeasurable — the $200 token may be cheap insurance even where evidence says marginal. (The play resolves this by taking both: the build AND the cert.)

3The evaluation frame — your axes, your doctrine

This evolved mid-project, driven by you. It governs every verdict on this page, including the battle plan.

AxisThe question
1 · First-gate recognitionWill a generalist staffing recruiter recognize the name when keyword-scanning or hearing it on a screen? (Your funnel: 0 interviews from 47+ cold applies; the recruiter gate is the wall.)
2 · Claim licensingDoes it put the exact tool/cert name legitimately on the page and in your mouth?
3 · The 30-hour bar (often dominant)Your doctrine: ~30 real hands-on hours = full-chest claiming on a live call. No hours, no claim, regardless of ability. So: is hands-on access even AVAILABLE to an individual, and what do 30 hours produce?
4 · Interview survivabilityDoes 30 hours + the artifact hold up with someone who actually knows the tool?
5 · Joy = mastery toward the goalYour correction, verbatim spirit: the joy is in the goal, the work, and the mastery — never the content. A course qualifies by being legit and effective, not fun.
The founding case: Crawford

The engine's record + your clarification: the Airtable loss didn't happen on the resume. It happened on the screening call — the recruiter couldn't evaluate capability, only comfort, and your comfort was honestly capped because you hadn't technically logged the hours. "If I did take a 30 hour course I would just gone for it."

Implication that reshaped everything: the highest-value purchases are permission slips — cheap, legit ways to log the hours that make the honest claim available. The paper is secondary. Your doctrine and the market's cert-silence (§1) agree completely.
