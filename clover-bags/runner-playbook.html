The one job

Get people signed up, draw partners fair and random, keep two boards moving, run the 4-Bagger Pot with some theater, and make sure everyone — especially the newcomer who's never thrown a bag — leaves wanting to come back Monday.

01

The 60-second version

Monday night. Anyone who walks in can sign up before we start. We randomly draw partners (you don't bring a teammate — you get one), play a double-elimination bracket on two boards, and somebody wins the pot. Alongside it runs the 4-Bagger Pot — a $1-ticket raffle with a carryover jackpot that's the night's big moment.

02

Why blind draw — the whole point

This is the thing that made Wednesday darts last 8 years, and it's why we run bags the same way. Random partners means:

  • Two ringers can't stack a team and run the table every week.
  • A brand-new player always feels like they've got a shot — because they might draw a great partner.
  • The good players accept they should win even carrying a weak partner — so they buy in too.
  • The newer players get better fast, playing alongside strong ones. Give it a few months and nobody's bad. The room becomes a crew, not a clique.
Say it out loud to newcomers

"You don't need a partner — you'll get drawn one. Anybody can win this." That single sentence is the best recruiting tool you have. Comp a newcomer's entry if it helps get the numbers even.

This is also why we run real rules, not watered-down ones (Section 05). The darts night pulled in serious city-league players precisely because it was legit. Rigorous game, welcoming room — that's the combination.

04

Sign-up & the draw

Timing

Open sign-ups when the night starts and close them before the first game — same as darts. Once it's closed, you draw. Don't hold the bracket waiting on stragglers; let them catch next week (or slot them into the loser's bracket only if you haven't started — your call).

Singles or doubles? (the same rule as darts)

Players signed upFormat
8 or more, even numberBlind-draw doubles — randomly pair everyone into teams.
Fewer than 8, or an odd numberRun it as singles (1v1).
You're the flex

Just like Sean steps out of darts to make the numbers work, the runner (or a willing regular) plays or sits to keep it even and teamable. Odd number at 9? Comp a newcomer to make it 10, or sit yourself to make 8. Getting to an even 8+ unlocks doubles, which is the better night.

The draw

Let Scoreholio do the random pairing (or draw names from a cup if you're going old-school). The randomness has to be visible and fair — that's the whole social contract. Announce the teams out loud; people love hearing who they got.

05

The rules card

This is the real cornhole spine. Print this section and tape it by the boards — it settles 95% of disputes. All RULE unless marked.

🌽 How to play — Clover Bags

Scoring (cancellation)

  • Bag through the hole = 3 points. Bag resting on the board = 1. On the ground (or touched ground first) = 0.
  • Each round, only one team scores. Total each team's points for the round; the higher subtracts the lower and scores the difference. Tie = nobody scores.
  • Example: You get 1 in the hole (3) + 1 on the board (1) = 4. They get 2 on the board (2). You score 4 − 2 = 2. They score 0.

Winning

  • First to 21 or more at the end of a full round wins. Going over is fine — 23 wins. No "bust," no win-by-2. (Fastest, fewest arguments.)
  • The win is only checked after all 8 bags are thrown — never mid-round.

Throwing

  • Each player throws 4 bags, alternating one at a time with the opponent.
  • Last team to score throws first the next round ("honors"). If nobody scored, same team throws first again.
  • Foul (bag is dead, removed): stepping on/over the front edge of the board before releasing; a bag that hits the floor first then slides on; a bag that hits the ceiling, a light, or a TV.
  • A bag legally on the board that gets knocked into the hole later by another bag counts — final resting position is what matters.

Doubles setup

  • Partners stand diagonally — one at each board. The two players at the same board are opponents. You stay at your board the whole game.

Speed lever runner's call

  • Standard game is to 21. On a packed night with a long bracket, drop to 15 to clear games faster. Decide before the round starts, announce it, keep it consistent.
House-rule trap

The "bust back to 13 if you go over 21" rule is fun but slows games and starts arguments — no pro league uses it. Skip it for the tournament. If the crowd begs for it, run it as a one-off "house night" and post the reset number first.

06

Running the bracket on 2 boards

We start with two boards (more when the patio's built). That's the real constraint, so the night is designed around it. Good news: bags games are quick, and two boards means two matches at once.

The format

  • Double elimination — two losses and you're out. This guarantees everyone at least two games, which is the single most important thing for a night people come back to. Nobody drives over, loses once, and leaves mad.
  • One game per match (to 21), best-of-3 for the final only. Single games keep the bracket moving on two boards.

Field size

12 players is the sweet spot; ~16 is the cap on two boards before benched people get bored. Past 16, either start a waitlist for next week or split into two tiers. When the patio adds boards, raise the cap.

Keeping the energy up while people wait

  • Put the live bracket/leaderboard on a TV (Clover's got plenty). Benched players are drinking and watching — that's fine for a bar, as long as they can see what's next.
  • Use a 20-minute soft cap per match; if a game's dragging, call it (highest score wins, or one sudden-death round).
  • Call the next two pairs out loud so they're ready the second a board opens. Dead board time is the enemy.

08

The 4-Bagger Pot the night's big moment

This is the bags version of the darts Hot Shot — same structure, same theater, same carryover drama. It's the thing people remember and the reason the pot grows week over week.

How it runs

  1. Sell $1 tickets all night (separate from the entry). Keep a running pot total visible by the boards.
  2. At a good moment (between rounds, crowd's into it), draw tickets in sequence — same escalation as the darts Hot Shot:
    • 1st draw: winner gets a free drink.
    • 2nd draw: winner gets a shot.
    • 3rd draw — the 4-Bagger Challenge: 🔇 music off, whole bar watching. The drawn player gets one round — 4 bags — at the board. Each bag in the hole pays ¼ of the pot. Sink all four (a true 4-bagger) and they take the whole pot.
  3. Whatever isn't won carries over to next Monday. A fat carryover pot is your best "come back next week" hook — text it to the list.
  4. Optional 4th draw: one more name, single-bag attempt at a fixed payout if the main challenge whiffed.
The ritual is the point

Sean's darts tip, straight across: kill the music, build it up, make the room watch. The 4-Bagger Challenge is theater. Balance paying out (exciting now) against letting it carry (anticipation later) — a growing jackpot is its own marketing.

Second money line — pick one to start

A 50/50 raffle or a $10 mulligan (re-throw) sale runs in parallel, near-pure-profit, and funds prizes / the bar's cut. Start with whichever's simpler for you to run and we'll tune it.

09

Juice & growth

Season standings — the come-back hook

Beyond one night's pot, track weekly placement points (e.g. 1st = 5, 2nd = 3, 3rd = 2, 4th = 1) on a board or in the app, building to a season champ. This is what turns a fun night into a habit — regulars come back to climb the standings. Real Chicago bar leagues live on this ("every night has a winner, every season has a champ"). PROPOSED enhancement — worth the tracking effort?

Recruiting (where the players come from)

  • Wednesday darts regulars — same competitive-social crowd, different night. Your warmest list. Text them.
  • Monday's existing Service Industry Night crowd — industry folks are off Mondays and already in the room (Happy Hour 4–7 + 40% off w/ proof). Pair the bags night with it.
  • The sports/Iowa crowd Clover already draws.
  • Capture contacts at every sign-up and group-text the carryover pot before Mondays — Sean's #1 darts-recruiting move.

When the patio opens

More space = more boards = bigger field. Raise the cap, run more matches in parallel, and the same model scales (Scoreholio's free plan handles up to 8 courts). This is also the moment to consider a second tier or a bigger "major" night.

Troubleshooting / quick answers

SituationWhat to do
Odd number of playersYou (or a regular) sit or play to make it even. Comp a newcomer to get to an even 8+ for doubles. Or run singles.
Way too many players (>16)Drop games to 15 to clear faster, start a waitlist, or split into two flights. Don't let benched people sit 40 minutes.
A game's dragging past the capCall the 20-minute cap: highest score wins, or one sudden-death round.
Scoring disputePoint to the rules card (Section 05). Cancellation + count out loud before clearing the boards prevents most of these.
A bag's in/out call is contestedOn the board surface = 1, through the hole = 3, touched the ground first = dead. If truly 50/50, re-throw that one bag.
App dies mid-nightSwitch to the printed PrintYourBrackets sheet and pen. Keep it on the table as backup every week.
No-show after sign-up/drawIf pre-game, re-draw. If mid-bracket, that team forfeits the current game; use a sub from the bench if everyone agrees.
