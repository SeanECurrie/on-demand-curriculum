Overview¶

Charter (split made explicit 2026-07-02, resolving audit fork F4): this index is the catalog — the canonical per-document cards and master table; methodology.md is the narrative layer. Doc-by-doc detail lives here and only here.

The methodology layer is reference infrastructure, not working documentation. Skills reference these documents; they do not duplicate their content. New methodology documents require operator approval and must follow the existing format: problem statement, methodology, binary self-tests, anti-pattern companions. A precedence rule (methodology-immutable rule 5, added 2026-06-12) governs divergence: when a skill and its companion methodology document diverge on operational specifics (thresholds, IDs, procedures), the skill is the operational source of truth and the methodology document carries rationale and history – divergences are defects to reconcile toward the skill, not licenses to pick either.

The methodology/ directory also contains two subdirectories: research/ (supplementary research artifacts including agent-architecture-landscape.md, which the 24 census count includes) and walkthroughs/ (walkthrough-related materials). The cards below index the top-level core documents.

By Domain¶

Research Methodology¶

credibility-tiers.md¶

What it covers: A 5-tier source weighting system for evaluating research sources. Every finding gets tagged with its source credibility tier, making it explicit how much confidence a claim deserves. The tiers run from Tier 1 (official documentation and verified changelogs – highest weight) through Tier 5 (general community sentiment – direction indicator only). The document includes a decision tree for assigning tiers and rules for when to upgrade or downgrade a source’s effective tier based on context.

Key concepts: 5-tier source hierarchy, tier assignment decision tree, tier upgrading/downgrading, confidence-weighted evidence, the principle that contradictions between tiers are intelligence insights rather than failures.

Companion skill: research-engine (applies tiers during research), findings-formatter (embeds tier tags in output).

When loaded: Referenced during any research operation. The editorial-standards document requires tier tagging on every claim. The dual-source-intelligence methodology uses tiers to weight contradictions. The research-cadence-template uses tiers to set confidence-based staleness decay rates.

dual-source-intelligence.md¶

What it covers: A research discipline that combines official documentation (via Context7 MCP) with community intelligence (via Bright Data MCP) to produce higher-fidelity findings than either source alone. Documentation tells you how something is supposed to work; community intelligence tells you how it actually works. The gap between those two is where the most valuable findings live. Defines three operating modes: documentation pull (Context7-led), community sweep (Bright Data-led), and full dual-source (both sources required per claim).

Key concepts: Dual-source validation, documentation-community gap as intelligence insight, three operating modes (documentation pull, community sweep, full dual-source), the principle that neither official nor community sources are sufficient alone.

Companion skill: research-engine (executes the dual-source protocol), depth-governor (determines which mode based on depth classification).

When loaded: Referenced during any research operation that uses MCP tools. The depth-assessment document determines how many claims require full dual-sourcing (scan: pivotal claims only; deep-dive: all claims). The editorial-standards document’s “Community Validation” principle (principle 4) derives from this methodology.

depth-assessment.md¶

What it covers: A mandatory 30-second classification step at the start of any pipeline stage that determines whether to operate at scan depth (breadth-first) or deep-dive depth (depth-first). Prevents the default LLM behavior of medium-quality everything. Three classification questions determine depth: (1) Will this finding drive a GO/NO-GO decision? (2) Is the operator new to this topic? (3) Has this topic had security implications? The first YES answer triggers deep-dive. All NOs means scan is appropriate. Mixed-depth within a single stage is normal and expected.

Key concepts: Scan vs. deep-dive classification, 30-second forcing function, mixed-depth as the normal case, the distinction between coverage (scan goal) and confidence (deep-dive goal), the principle that even scan depth requires dual-source validation for pivotal claims.

Companion skill: depth-governor (enforces the classification at pipeline stage boundaries).

When loaded: Referenced at the start of every pipeline stage during governed operations. The binary-self-tests document has paired quick/thorough test variants calibrated to scan/deep-dive depth. The section-construction methodology activates only at deep-dive depth. The findings-pattern-library allows lightweight templates at scan depth.

findings-pattern-library.md¶

What it covers: A concept-to-structure mapping table for research output. When the engine synthesizes findings, it selects from 7 validated structures rather than inventing format each time. The patterns are: Contradiction Insight (sources disagree on a factual claim), Validation Entry (hypothesis confirmed), Discovery Entry (new finding), Risk Entry (risk assessment), Comparison Matrix (tool/option comparison), Resolution Entry (question resolved), and Recommendation Entry (actionable recommendation). Each pattern has required and optional fields with source and credibility tier requirements per claim.

Key concepts: 7 finding types with distinct structures, concept-type determines structure (not prose defaults), required vs. optional fields scaled by depth, the principle that patterns are advisory with an escape hatch for genuine edge cases.

Companion skill: findings-formatter (applies the correct pattern structure to research output).

When loaded: Referenced during synthesis stages of any research operation. The editorial-standards document requires source and tier tagging on every field. The depth-assessment document determines whether to use full or lightweight pattern templates.

research-cadence-template.md¶

What it covers: A framework for designing scheduled intelligence refresh cadences to prevent knowledge base staleness. Defines three threshold types for determining when findings can no longer be trusted: time-based (days since last verification, scaled by domain volatility), event-based (specific triggers that invalidate findings regardless of age), and confidence-based (source tier decay rates – Tier 1 findings decay slowly, Tier 5 findings decay fastest). Each engine configures its own schedule based on domain volatility, cost of stale data, and operational budget.

Key concepts: Staleness thresholds (time-based, event-based, confidence-based), domain volatility classification (critical/high/medium/low), source tier decay rates, the principle that intelligence decays and refresh must be scheduled rather than memory-triggered.

Companion skill: research-engine (executes refresh sweeps), market-scanner (landscape-level refresh).

When loaded: Referenced when configuring a new engine’s research schedule or when evaluating whether existing knowledge base entries are still trustworthy. The credibility-tiers document provides the tier definitions that feed confidence-based decay rates.

competitive-research-framework.md¶

What it covers: A standard, reusable 26-question battery to run against any target or competitor when building a competitive analysis, company briefing, or investment dossier. It exists so the research surface is consistent and complete rather than ad-hoc – every company gets asked the same dimensions (company & structure, product, pricing, positioning, customers, and so on), which keeps cross-competitor matrices clean and comparable. Each dimension runs under the dual-engine discipline (Bright Data first → WebSearch corroborator), credibility-tiered (T1 official → T5 self-reported), with claim-vs-independently-verified tagging. The battery is a minimum coverage checklist, not a maximum; dimensions that don’t apply are skipped with a one-line note rather than fabricated. Lifted 2026-06-20 from the competitive-analysis (MLR) engine’s competitive-research-brightdata skill (the migration of the registry competitive-research capability to OS level – a cabin lift of the question list; the heavier QA skill and deliverable templates remain candidates for a later cementing pass).

Key concepts: 26-dimension coverage battery, dual-engine discipline (primary + corroborator), credibility-tiering every answer, claim-vs-verified tagging, minimum-coverage-checklist (skip-with-note over fabricate), consistent question set as the enabler of comparable cross-competitor matrices.

Companion skill: market-scanner, company-briefing, research-engine (all reference the battery when scoping competitor/target research).

When loaded: Referenced when scoping research for a competitive analysis, company briefing, or investment dossier. Pairs with dual-source-intelligence (the corroboration discipline) and credibility-tiers (the tier definitions applied to each answer).

epistemic-tagging.md¶

What it covers: Per-claim confidence discipline – the structural backstop for Core Behavior #1 (“research before agreeing”). EngineOS already rated sources (credibility-tiers); this adds the missing layer for individual claims: (1) per-claim confidence tags – (VERIFIED) / (LIKELY) / (UNKNOWN) – riding the sentence, not the document; (2) an active-unknowns ledger that tracks what we don’t know as first-class objects with a resolution lifecycle; (3) the observation-beats-theory rule – a direct observation outranks documented theory. A decision that depends on an (UNKNOWN) must surface that dependency and suggest resolving it before committing. Promoted to OS Layer 1 on 2026-06-26 from the sandbox/gaming/ decision engine (D45, the first exercise of the sandbox promotion path) – the game engine, built for fun with nobody watching, independently engineered this backstop.

Key concepts: claim-level (not source-level) confidence, the three tags, unknowns as tracked objects with lifecycle, observation-beats-theory, the erosion failure mode (a (LIKELY) becomes a (VERIFIED) when a document flattens to uniform confident voice).

Companion skill: None directly – a discipline consumed by research and synthesis skills. Pairs with credibility-tiers (source axis ⊥ claim axis) and tone-firewall (the voice-level enforcement of the same honesty).

When loaded: Referenced during research synthesis and whenever a decision rides on an unverified claim. The sandbox charter (sandbox/README.md) documents its promotion provenance.

Quality Framework¶

binary-self-tests.md¶

What it covers: Concrete YES/NO tests that the engine can run against its own output to verify quality. Replaces aspirational quality language (“make it clear”) with testable checkpoints (“cover paragraph 3, read it without paragraph 1 – does it still track?”). Organized by pipeline stage: Research Stage Tests (R1-R4), Synthesis Stage Tests (S1-S4), Output Generation Tests (O1-O4), Visual Output Tests (V1-V6), and Cross-Cutting Tests (X1-X4, applied to everything). Each test has paired quick (scan depth) and thorough (deep-dive depth) variants. Includes test design rules: answerable YES/NO in under 5 seconds, references specific parts of output, a NO answer implies a specific fix.

Key concepts: Binary (YES/NO) quality verification, paired quick/thorough test variants, 4 test design rules, tests by pipeline stage (research, synthesis, output generation), the principle that LLMs can evaluate concrete yes/no questions but cannot reliably evaluate “is this good?” A canonical-battery note (2026-06-12) cross-maps the doc’s pipeline-stage batteries to the operational canonical batteries in .claude/skills/output-self-test/SKILL.md – new operational tests go to output-self-test; new rationale goes here.

Companion skill: output-self-test (executes binary tests against deliverables), sanity-check (uses binary tests for spot-checks).

When loaded: Referenced during QA stages of any governed operation. The phase-controller forces output-self-test invocation during skill chains. The depth-assessment document determines which test variant (quick or thorough) to run. Individual tests reference credibility-tiers and editorial-standards for their evaluation criteria.

anti-patterns.md¶

What it covers: A catalog of 15 real failure modes observed during production engine work. Every entry cites a specific incident – a file, a log entry, a session where something went wrong. Organized into Research Anti-Patterns, Output Anti-Patterns, and Process Anti-Patterns. Each entry has four components: what it looks like (recognition), why it happens (understanding the temptation), what good looks like (the alternative), and a binary self-test (catch it before it ships). Examples include The Understated Warning, Premature Confidence, The Settled Question That Was Not Settled, and The Half-Lifted Subsystem. (This card previously said 14 entries in Research/Production/Governance categories and cited an example entry that does not exist in the catalog – fixed 2026-07-02 against disk.)

Key concepts: 15 evidence-based failure modes, the requirement that every entry cite a real failure (no theoretical anti-patterns), framing as failure modes rather than commandments, binary self-tests as catch mechanisms, the principle that an engine that cannot name its own failure modes cannot improve.

Companion skill: sanity-check (its 11-mode spot-check gallery grew from the same incidents and overlaps this catalog; the two are maintained separately – operational spot-checks there, full rationale here).

When loaded: Referenced during QA operations and when the sanity-check skill detects a failure mode. The phase-controller can invoke sanity-check during forced chains. Skills that produce deliverables should be aware of the failure modes relevant to their output type.

adversarial-validation.md¶

What it covers: A two-agent testing methodology for validating composited systems. Formalized from the Cowork testing arc that validated EngineOS after the Act 3 build. Defines three roles (Executor, Evaluator, Operator), the independent context requirement (evaluator must not share the executor’s session), the communication protocol (operator routes between agents), and dual-layer test design (capability layer: does it produce good output; governance layer: did it follow its own process). Documents three structural failure modes of self-evaluation: sycophancy, keyword-passing, and count errors in self-audit.

Key concepts: Two-agent architecture (executor/evaluator/operator), independent context requirement, dual-layer testing (capability + governance), correction-of-the-corrector pattern, the principle that self-evaluation is structurally unreliable due to sycophancy, keyword-passing, and count errors.

Companion skill: adversarial-evaluator (spawns independent-context subagents for scored evaluation).

When loaded: Referenced when validating a completed build or when running adversarial evaluation on deliverables. The adversarial-evaluator skill implements this methodology. Used at major phase boundaries for critical work.

Production Methodology¶

section-construction.md¶

What it covers: A formalized incremental build process for complex outputs (interactive walkthroughs, comprehensive reports, multi-section deliverables). Prevents quality degradation from generating large outputs in single passes. Defines the phases: Phase A (plan sections with boundaries, cross-references, namespaces, and size estimates – presented for operator approval), Phase B (build one section at a time with cross-reference management and per-section binary self-tests), Phase C (full-output review for cross-reference integrity, convention consistency, and progressive complexity), Phase C.5 (render-validate loop – visual/interactive outputs only), and Phase D (Iterate – fold review findings back in). Activates only at deep-dive depth for outputs exceeding ~500 lines.

Key concepts: Phased incremental build (plan, build per-section, whole-output review, render-validate loop, iterate), namespace convention for IDs and anchors, cross-reference management, the gating question (“if I generate this entire output in one pass, will the last section be as good as the first?”), the ~32K token practical ceiling for coherent single-pass generation.

Companion skill: render-validate (verifies HTML output integrity after section construction).

When loaded: Referenced during production of complex deliverables classified at deep-dive depth. The depth-assessment document determines activation. The phase-controller includes section construction in skill chains for applicable output types.

factory-pattern.md¶

What it covers: Four variants of the “how does one system produce and maintain another” pattern, each with documented maintenance protocols. The variants on disk are: Full Extraction (complete capability lift with severed runtime dependency – the JSE-from-SEE case), Team Deployment (a configured workspace deployed for a team with different identity/access – the WondaKB case), Thin Routing (a capability stays at the OS and is reached through a routing skill rather than copied), and Instance Spawning (repeatable per-instance stand-up from a template). Each variant includes: when to use, ecosystem examples with citations, maintenance protocols with staleness indicators, and anti-patterns. The core problem addressed is that innovation flows outward from factories and never returns. (This card previously named three variants that do not exist in the document – fixed 2026-07-02 against disk.)

Key concepts: 4 factory variants (full extraction, team deployment, thin routing, instance spawning), reverse audit for back-porting product innovations, extraction manifests for provenance tracking, constitution drift checks, the principle that factories should absorb their products’ improvements.

Companion skill: None directly. Referenced by ecosystem-build methodology and the codebase-onboarder skill.

When loaded: Referenced when building new engines, migrating capabilities between engines, or auditing capability drift between a source and its deployed product. The ecosystem-build methodology uses the composite build variant. The lift-audit methodology checks for regressions introduced by factory operations.

editorial-standards.md¶

What it covers: Six core editorial principles governing how research findings, deployment guidance, and knowledge base entries are written, cited, and maintained: (1) Evidence Requirement – every claim needs a source and credibility tier, (2) Honest Assessment – never oversell, never downplay, note residual risk, (3) Transferable Framing – distinguish universal principles from tool-specific details, (4) Community Validation – documentation alone is insufficient, (5) Security as a Lens – evaluated on every decision not as a separate phase, (6) Living Documentation – mark outdated content, never delete. Also defines writing standards for tone, voice, formatting, and version-stamping.

Key concepts: 6 editorial principles, the evidence requirement (if you can’t cite it don’t claim it), residual risk as a mandatory element, transferable vs. tool-specific framing, security-at-every-step, living documentation with never-delete, the tone standard of “knowledgeable colleague, not manual or marketing page.”

Companion skill: output-self-test (tests against editorial standards), qa-pipeline (includes editorial checks).

When loaded: Referenced during any content production operation. The findings-pattern-library requires source and tier tagging per the evidence requirement. The binary-self-tests reference editorial standards in their evaluation criteria. Applies to all written output across all engines.

tone-firewall.md¶

What it covers: The rule that enthusiasm and assertion are two different voices governed by two different rules – never let hype attach to an unverified claim. Hype-voice (licensed): identity, vision, momentum – riffing about what a thing will be is a working tool. Claim-voice (gated): any specific factual assertion a decision will ride on – banned from hype-voice until verified per epistemic-tagging. Motivated enthusiasm is how a (LIKELY) becomes a (VERIFIED) by tone alone; the firewall lets the operator be genuinely enthusiastic without the enthusiasm corrupting the evidence base. Promoted to OS Layer 1 on 2026-06-26 from the sandbox gaming engine’s “Carl-Tone vs Claim-Tone Firewall” (D45) – the operator’s sharpest articulation of a discipline the professional corpus already gestured at.

Key concepts: hype-voice vs claim-voice, enthusiasm attaches only to the verified version of a thing, the unifying mechanism under feedback_positioning_not_marketing_voice, comprehension-design’s honesty-as-trust, and the D43 cheerleader-check.

Companion skill: None directly – a voice discipline consumed wherever deliverables or recommendations are written. Pairs with editorial-standards (principle 2, honest assessment) and epistemic-tagging (the claim-state machinery it enforces at the voice level).

When loaded: Referenced when producing any persuasive or enthusiasm-bearing output – positioning documents, briefings, recommendations – and when reviewing output for marketing-voice drift.

company-briefing.md¶

What it covers: The production playbook for a research-backed, single-file interactive HTML walkthrough about a specific target company, produced for a specific audience walking into a decision or conversation about that company. Formalized 2026-06-10 from three production instances: an engagement-prep briefing (2026-05-19), a walking-in briefing (2026-05-31 – introduced the 7-thread orthogonal dispatch), and a competitive-analysis dossier (2026-06-10). Defines a five-phase pipeline: Phase 1 (intake + depth classification, ending at a hard operator gate before dispatch), Phase 2 (research dispatch across orthogonal threads), Phase 3 (synthesis + tier coding), Phase 4 (design + build), Phase 5 (QA + deploy). Includes the intake structure (with a landing-page card toggle added 2026-06-12 – on by default, operator can decline; deploy-verification criterion C6 is conditioned on this setting so a card-less build on instruction still passes), the dimension-split heuristic for thread design, tool assignment per dimension, an adversarial criteria template, the GitHub Pages deploy convention, 5 anti-patterns, and binary self-tests. Covers orchestration only – substantive research methodology lives in the donor engines (SEE for credibility tiers + dual-source intelligence, ODC for the HTML output pattern + deploy convention).

Key concepts: Deliverable archetype (the briefing is the output – not a system-walkthrough explaining EngineOS), orthogonal research dispatch (typically 6-8 threads, 100+ tier-coded sources; N adapts – orthogonal coverage is the principle, not a fixed thread count), hard Gate 0 before dispatch (scope drift after parallel threads have run is asymmetrically expensive), audience-is-always-specific, the “how this was built” meta-section.

Companion skill: company-briefing (orchestrates the pipeline). The framework pattern patterns/frameworks/company-briefing.md abstracts it.

When loaded: Referenced when the operator names a target company, a specific audience, and requests an interactive HTML output. The depth-assessment document governs Phase 1 classification; credibility-tiers and dual-source-intelligence govern Phases 2-3; section-construction and render-validate govern Phases 4-5.

comprehension-design.md¶

What it covers: The parent standard for audience-facing deliverables: calibrate the form of a deliverable to the specific human reading it, so the substance lands – without simplifying the substance. It is the opposite of dumbing down – honesty and rigor made legible IS the trust signal, and EngineOS’s documented failure mode is under-presenting rigor. The flowchart pattern (patterns/problems/connective-visual-language-for-nontechnical-buyin.md) is one instance of this parent standard; the standard applies to every audience-facing deliverable EngineOS produces. Established 2026-06-21 (operator-named off the flowchart canon during an investor-diligence audit), grounded in a 6-agent dual-engine research audit (findings at docs/plans/2026-06-21-comprehension-design-findings.md) that converges across intelligence, medicine, consulting, and cognitive science.

Key concepts:

  • form-to-human calibration without simplifying substance; honesty-as-trust-signal
  • the documented failure mode is under-presenting rigor, not overwhelming the reader
  • the toolkit: answer-first (A), magnitude→visual / dual-coding (B), honesty as the trust engine (C), make-it-stick via active production (D), progressive disclosure (E), audience calibration (F)
  • §G (ratified 2026-07-01): the returning-stakeholder surface – a managed, ongoing engagement gets a “since your last look” front door so a returning reader sees what changed and why before re-reading anything

Companion skill: section-construction, render-validate, framework-explainer, company-briefing (all audience-facing production skills calibrate form to the named human per this standard).

When loaded: Referenced when producing any audience-facing deliverable – before deciding its form, structure, and visual language for the named audience. Pairs with editorial-standards (voice/quality) and the connective-visual-language problem pattern (one concrete instance).

engagement-workup.md¶

What it covers: The playbook for EngineOS’s core client-engagement motion: someone brings a situation (intake, brain-dump, brief, voicemail, transcript); we research it, synthesize it, and return something that surfaces what matters – or execute the thing and systematize it. A 4-type router: Type A (decision-support briefing), Type B (intake-response synthesis), Type C (execute + systematize), Type D (capability-setup). Subject-agnostic by doctrine – a cornhole tournament and a pharma diligence brief run the identical motion (a go-to-market launch engagement ≡ a competitive-analysis dossier). Includes the person-card firewall (candor calibrates strategy; it is never quoted back), the audio-intake path (transcribe → transcript-processor), and step 6’s iteration lifecycle co-owned with the SME-iteration loop (patterns/frameworks/sme-iteration-loop.md): the 3 feedback flavors (direction / insider fact / reasoned disagreement), the adjudication table, the early ground-truth gate, and the context handoff. Extracted constitution-from-reality from the EngineOS-era arc (a comp-benchmarking engagement → a walking-in briefing → two competitive-analysis dossiers → a go-to-market launch engagement → an investor-diligence engagement + the Cloudflare execute-and-systematize motion), D42; cold-start culmination test passed live on a real 26-minute audio intake (2026-06-25).

Key concepts: the 4-type router, don’t-ask-twenty-questions-first (clarifying questions are a deliverable at the end, not a gate at the start), subject-agnostic traversal via doctrine not domain expertise, the person-card firewall, the SME-iteration loop with its early ground-truth gate, engagement-artifact structure (name=identity, state=field; snap-across-the-gate vs iterate-within).

Companion skill: engagement-workup (the orchestrator / cold-session entry point). Framework patterns: engagement-workup, sme-iteration-loop, engagement-artifact-structure.

When loaded: Referenced when an engagement fires (“here’s an intake from [name]”, “do a workup on X”, a voicemail/transcript arrives). The EN chain in phase-controller routes through it; transcribe and transcript-processor feed it.

System Building¶

ecosystem-build.md¶

What it covers: The three-act audit-to-composite methodology for building a unified system from a multi-project ecosystem. Formalized from the actual EngineOS build. Act 1 (Structural Reconnaissance) maps what exists without judging it – produces ecosystem map, growth story, deep attention register, and Act 2 workspace. Act 2 (Exhaustive Discovery) reads every line using capability-domain reading – produces capability cards, skill genealogy, cross-cutting analyses. Act 3 (Build from Evidence) composites best versions – five-phase build with evaluator-embedded checkpoint reviews, registry system, constitution written after the build.

Key concepts: Three-act architecture (reconnaissance, discovery, build), capability-domain reading (same capability across all projects simultaneously), 70% reading rule, best-version-wins compositing (not selection), standing research methodology (research during reads, not deferred), genealogy synthesis as first-class output, constitution-from-reality (written after the build, not before).

Companion skill: codebase-onboarder (uses the reconnaissance posture for new codebase orientation).

When loaded: Referenced when building a new OS-level system from multiple sources, conducting ecosystem-wide audits, or migrating engine capabilities to the OS layer. The lift-audit methodology is its post-build companion. The adversarial-validation methodology tests the system it produces.

lift-audit.md¶

What it covers: A three-dimensional audit methodology for detecting (a) internal regressions from lift operations and (b) external currency gaps where industry has evolved past the lifted baseline. The three dimensions are: Dimension 1 (Internal Regression Check – what ported vs. what exists in source, using method-level parity as primary signal), Dimension 2 (External Currency Validation – is the lifted baseline still current industry practice, using Context7 and Bright Data), Dimension 3 (Refactor-vs-Regression Distinction – when target differs from source, is it regression or legitimate cleanup). Formalized from real incidents: Attio direct-API never-lifted regression (891 lines at source, zero in target), notion-ops 55% body reduction, notion-mapper orphaned-skill regression.

Key concepts: Three-dimensional audit (regression, currency, refactor-vs-regression), method-level parity verification (not just line-count comparison), body-size delta as secondary-only evidence, orphaned-skill detection, the principle that regression and currency gaps accumulate invisibly and surface only when exercised.

Companion skill: sanity-check (spot-check for orphaned skills and missing implementations).

When loaded: Referenced after any major lift or migration operation. Companion to ecosystem-build (the construction methodology it audits) and adversarial-validation (the evaluation posture it inherits). Used during engine migration to EngineOS.

planning-enforcement.md¶

What it covers: When planning skills (brainstorming, writing-plans) are required, when they are optional, and how the enforcement model works. Documents the empirical evidence: planning skills were skipped in three consecutive sessions despite being specified in phase-controller chains. Defines two enforcement types: structural enforcement (planner output is a required input to the production step – Layer 4 of the run plan must be populated) and advisory enforcement with transparency (skip-justification required in the run plan, visible at the operator approval gate). Maps enforcement per chain type (BL and EW require both; EN-deliverable and CP require writing-plans only; research and maintenance require neither).

Key concepts: Two enforcement types (structural and advisory-with-transparency), the asymmetry is intentional (structural for concrete artifacts, advisory for process value), chain-by-chain enforcement mapping, the root cause that agents conflate “I know what to build” with “I’ve thought through how to build it,” the principle that detailed operator prompts create an illusion that scoping was already done.

Companion skill: phase-controller (enforces planning requirements per chain type).

When loaded: Referenced by the phase-controller during chain execution. The run plan Layer 4 (Design + Plan) is the structural enforcement mechanism. Applies to BL, EW, EN-deliverable, and CP operation types.

cross-operator-distribution.md¶

memory-architecture.md¶

  • D-2 locks root-only logging with engine tags; D-3 sets CONTEXT-HISTORY archival triggers; D-4 defines registry ID scope; D-5 locks the canonical log schemas (engine column on every row).

Closes the capture-without-retrieval gap (zero reads of knowledge/concepts/*.md across ~30 hours of substantive work before the fix).
  • Each decision and the retrieval layer are backed by external research (44+ sources across D-1..D-5; retrieval anchored in DECISIONS.md D32).

Key concepts: 8-surface memory hierarchy with one-job-per-surface, root-only log model with engine tags (not per-tenant logs), CONTEXT-HISTORY archival triggers (size and time), the binary test for surface selection (“which surface’s one job matches this information?”), the principle that surface role confusion (a surface doing two jobs) is a violation to correct, three-layer retrieval architecture (session-start / mid-work hook / manual escalation), six-surface priority corpus with signal-to-noise ordering, stopping rule (surfaces 1-2 yielding 3+ strong hits halts sweep of lower surfaces), the principle that capture without retrieval is a write-only system, why structured markdown + ripgrep beats vector retrieval at personal-KB scale (<2000 articles).

people-profiling.md¶

What it covers: The canon for EngineOS’s People Profiling subsystem – a cross-cutting OS capability (Layer 1), not an engine, for profiling the people the operator works with:

  • The core principle: a person is several independent axes, not one rank – collapsing them is how a friend’s offhand take becomes system canon.
  • The six axes: Tier (importance, operator-set, sticky) · Heat (activity, computed by scripts/people-heat.py) · Influence (does their input count as canon? – canon / domain-authority / lessons-only / NOT-canon) · Value-type · Relationship-direction · Sensitivity.
  • Tier ⊥ Heat ⊥ Influence, and Influence is a SAFETY axis (important-to-operator ≠ right-for-the-system) – enforced loudly via a profile caveat block and prompt-retrieval.py echoing ⚠ NOT-CANON on mention.
  • The five disciplines: operator is the gate; draft-and-react; append-only + evidence-tagged + pointer-based; everyone accounted for; bidirectional canon. Plus the candidate-ranking rule (Core/Active-thin → untiered-high-heat → Reference-suppressed).
  • Provenance: re-lifted from the SEE source on 2026-06-24 after the original compositing left it engine-bound (only the operator self-profile came over) – engine-coupling it again would repeat that incomplete-lift bug.

Key concepts: six independent profiling axes, Tier ⊥ Heat ⊥ Influence, Influence as a safety axis (NOT-canon enforcement), operator-is-the-gate, draft-and-react over interrogate, append-only/evidence-tagged/pointer-based profiles, everyone-accounted-for (pointer-cards minimum), bidirectional canon (agent may notice and propose; operator rules), heat as booster/tiebreaker not sort key, the review-and-adjust dossier as the steering surface.
