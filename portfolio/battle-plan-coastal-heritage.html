01

TL;DR, seven things to know walking in

4 min read

If you read nothing else, read this. Each line is unpacked later.

  1. It is a small, conservative, single-market community bank. Roughly $985M in assets, ~$782M deposits, about 10 branches across the Massachusetts South Shore, mutually owned (no shareholders, depositor-owned), ~84 staff, chartered 1889 T1. Decisions are relationship-led and risk-averse. Sell trust and the franchise, not a feature list.
  2. They run a classic small-bank stack. COCC core and digital banking, Fiserv bill-pay and P2P, Zelle live, mobile apps with biometric login T1. Capable, but there is no public sign of adaptive, risk-based authentication, behavioral analytics, or real-time transaction risk scoring T4.
  3. There is no named CISO. The public org has a Chief Risk Officer, a CIO, and a real BSA/AML function, but no dedicated security chief T1. Fraud and security sit on already-stretched people. That is the opening.
  4. They had a data-security notice this year. A 2026 incident was disclosed; the scale and cause are not public, so do not speculate T1 T2. What it means: fraud and security are live, board-level conversations right now.
  5. The macro forcing function is check fraud. FinCEN check-fraud reports nearly doubled, from ~350K (2021) to ~680K (2022), and the agency's own data says small and mid-size banks file the majority T1. A bank this size is the bullseye.
  6. The compliance clock is dated. Nacha's new credit-push-fraud rules put a fraud-monitoring obligation on receiving banks for the first time, phasing in through 2026 T1. With Zelle live, that is a requirement, not an option.
  7. Your wedge is consolidation. One platform across adaptive auth, real-time transaction risk, and external-threat takedown, with a managed SOC. Exactly what a resource-thin bank with no CISO cannot build in-house.
▸ The single most useful frame

Coastal Heritage Bank does not need another point tool. It needs the fraud-and-security function it cannot afford to staff. Sell the platform-plus-SOC consolidation, not a feature. Internalize that and everything else in this plan lines up behind it.

Why now, the threat landscape and the clock

Two things create urgency: the threats are rising fast, and the rules now put dated obligations on a bank this size. Use the macro to open, then localize it.

▸ The dated compliance clock (this is the real lever)

FFIEC authentication guidance (2021): expects layered security and treats single-factor authentication as inadequate for digital access T1. Nacha credit-push-fraud rules: for the first time, receiving banks carry a fraud-monitoring obligation, phasing in through 2026 T1. FinCEN deepfake / GenAI alert (Nov 2024): synthetic identity documents defeating onboarding checks T1. A receiving bank with Zelle live now has a dated, regulator-set requirement, not an option.

The liability direction matters more than any single case. The CFPB sued over Zelle fraud (alleging more than $870M in customer losses), then voluntarily dismissed that suit with prejudice in March 2025 T1. Do not cite the $870M as a finding; it was an allegation in a case that did not proceed. The durable signal is the one-way direction of travel: regulators and the courts keep pushing more of the loss toward the institution.

▸ The trust argument, quantified

In a 2025 ABA / Morning Consult survey, consumers ranked banks first for fraud protection by a wide margin over every other industry T2. For a single-market mutual, trust is the whole franchise. One local fraud story does damage a megabank could absorb and this bank cannot. That is your emotional close on top of the regulatory one.

▸ Be precise, do not over-claim

Two honesty rules that keep you credible with a technical buyer. First, on behavioral biometrics: Appgate orchestrates behavioral signals into its risk engine and returns a combined score; it is not marketed as a first-party behavioral-biometrics product, so say "we ingest and score behavioral signals," not "we have behavioral biometrics" T2. Second, Appgate's real strength is breadth across the fraud chain (external takedown + transaction risk + adaptive auth) with a managed SOC, not best-of-breed depth in any single scoring engine. Sell the consolidation, not a head-to-head on a pure-play's home turf.

The objection you must pre-empt

5 min read

There is one objection that will surface in any bank's vendor-risk review, and it is better coming from you first.

▸ The fact, stated cleanly

Appgate filed a prepackaged Chapter 11 on May 6, 2024, and emerged debt-free and privately held under Magnetar Capital (about $13.4B in assets under management) roughly two months later T1 T2. A community bank's third-party-risk process will find this. Raise it before they do.

The defensible narrative, all sourced:

  • It was a fast, consensual prepackaged recapitalization backed by nearly all creditors, not a liquidation and not a fire sale T1 T2.
  • The company emerged debt-free in under two months, with customer commitments intact T1.
  • It is now backed by a $13B-plus institutional investor and has invested through 2025 and 2026 (rebrand, analyst recognition, new board members) T1.
  • A former Discover Financial Services CEO joined the board in late 2025, a financial-services credibility signal a banker will recognize T1.

The honest watch-item: as a private company it no longer files public financials, so be ready for the "less financial transparency" follow-up. Offer what you can (references, the SOC's track record, the Magnetar backing) rather than dodging it. Stability since mid-2024 is the stronger story than anything before it.

10

Discovery and the first meeting

7 min read

Questions that open the room

  • On the recent incident: after this year's data-security notice, what actually changed in how you authenticate logins and monitor transactions, versus what you told customers?
  • On real-time money: with Zelle live, how do you score a transfer for risk before it settles, given there is no clawback?
  • On ownership: with no dedicated security chief, who runs fraud monitoring day to day, and how stretched is that risk-and-BSA team?
  • On the clock: Nacha's receiving-bank monitoring obligation is phasing in this year; where are you on it?
  • On brand: if a phishing site spoofs your login page tonight, how fast can you get it taken down today?

▸ When to qualify the deal down

If they already run Verafin or Abrigo deeply for AML and they have recently overhauled authentication, the wedge narrows to Brand Guardian. That is still a real deal, just a smaller one. Size it as a takedown-and-monitoring engagement and do not force a platform rip; the relationship is worth more than the quota math on a fight you will lose.

  • Employee count (~84) is a single aggregator source, so treat it as directional.
  • The core vendor (COCC) is inferred from a digital-banking URL fingerprint, not named on the bank's site.
  • Appgate's behavioral story is signal orchestration into a risk engine, not a first-party biometrics product. Do not overclaim it.
  • There is no confirmed public US-community-bank logo for Appgate's fraud line; the credit-union reference is the closest.
  • The account-takeover, push-payment, and synthetic-identity dollar figures are analyst models (Javelin, Deloitte, TransUnion), not regulator counts. They are attributed as such.
▸ The honest version

This plan tells you where to look and what to ask, not what is certain. Verify the load-bearing items live (their current authentication posture, whether an AML incumbent is already in place, the specifics of the 2026 notice) before you build the forecast on them. The value here is that you walk in informed and credible, not that every line is gospel.
