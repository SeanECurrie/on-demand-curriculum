a · One health-provider check before anything else (free)

Because you're a therapist, not a shoe storeStandard marketing advice says "slap a tracking pixel on your site." For a therapy practice that advice is a liability, not a tip. Regulators treat a visit to a therapist's website as health information, and the usual ad trackers (Meta Pixel, plain Google Analytics) can't legally carry it. The makers won't sign the agreement (a BAA, the data-handling contract) that would make it OK. This is the exact thing the FTC fined BetterHelp ($7.8M), Cerebral, and Monument for. The four ideas behind that, named once

Cost per client, per channel: spend ÷ clients who actually booked and showed (not clicks, not leads). The denominator is the whole game.

Last-touch is a trap: a friend refers someone, they Google your name, click your ad, and book; the naive setup credits the ad for a client the referral earned. The two intake questions fix it for free.

07The de-risk you'll actually like

The staffing hit exposed something real: income riding on other people's labor. First, the reframe: somewhere between a third and a half of clinicians turn over every year in this field (30–60% is the range). That's the baseline, not bad luck. The practices that absorb it are simply built to. SECTOR DATA · T2 You asked what "a structured supervision pipeline" even means, so plainly:

PlainlyA supervision pipeline is how a licensed psychologist turns her single license into a team's worth of billable care. You bring on clinicians who are fully trained but not yet independently licensed, still earning the supervised hours the state requires, and they see clients under your oversight, billed under your credential. Because you supply the supervision they're legally required to have, the practice keeps a larger share of what they bill. You earn on their work without spending your own client hours, and because they need your supervision to get licensed, they tend to stay and grow into senior staff.
◆ Why it's your highest-alignment move
Revenue that isn't a clinical hour of yours. Group-supervise pre-licensed associates, 6–8 at ~$40–75 each is $240–600 for one two-hour block, plus the margin on their client billings (their split is lower precisely because you provide required supervision). FEE + SPLIT DATA · T2
A pipeline of clinicians who stay. They need your hours to get licensed, stickier than a lateral hire, cheaper while they ramp, and they grow into the staff that fills your caseload. Replacing a clinician runs 50–200% of salary; a renewable inflow is the antidote.
The work you're best at becomes the moat. The mentoring you're already good at is the engine that both diversifies income and hardens the practice against exactly the shock you just took.
The Illinois detail we checked so you don't have to: in IL the trainee can not pay you for supervision, or the hours don't count and the insurer won't pay. The model is "employ them, earn margin, supervise as part of the job." Blue Cross IL does reimburse trainee sessions billed under your credential (with limits: up to 4 trainees, you available 100% of the time). 225 ILCS 15 + BCBSIL '25 · T1

If you control schedule/caseload and they depend on you for income, the IRS may call them an employee regardless of the contract, creating back-tax exposure.
