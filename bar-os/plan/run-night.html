A Monday, start to finish.

The night, minute by minute

How a Monday actually runs.

The clock, from doors to last bag. The two times that matter are locked: sign-up closes at 7, first bags fly at 7:30.

4:00

Happy Hour + Service Industry Night

Mondays already run Happy Hour 4–7 and Service Industry Night. The crowd's building — boards can go up whenever there's room.

by 6:45

Set up

Two boards out, taped at the line (same distance both lanes). Sign-up sheet and the 4-Bagger ticket cup on the bar where people walk in. Bracket ready on the app — with the printed bracket + pen as backup. Door poster up.

7:00

Sign-up closes

$10 a player, collected at sign-up. Grab a phone number or email from each — that's next week's list. Once it's closed, you draw; don't hold the bracket for stragglers.

7:10

Draw the partners

Pair everyone at random and announce the teams out loud — people love hearing who they got. Doubles if 8 or more and even; singles if fewer or odd. Set the double-elim bracket, first matches to both boards.

7:30

Bags fly

First throw. Games to 21 (drop to 15 on a packed night), cancellation scoring, two matches running at once. Double-elim means everyone plays at least twice — nobody drives over, loses once, and leaves.

all night

The 4-Bagger Pot runs alongside

Sell $1 tickets all night, pot total on a small board by the lanes. At a good moment, draw for it — the signature stop-the-room moment (see the money below).

close

Winners paid, photos grabbed

Winning team takes the pot; second gets their buy-in back. Grab two or three photos — the winners, a packed board, the 4-Bagger moment. That's the recap.

Tuesday

Post the recap

Winner + a photo, the next morning. It's the proof that powers the next week — the recap caption's already written in the Assets.

The rules, in brief

Real rules — no gatekeeping.

Legit cornhole, so the competition's real, but simple enough to teach a first-timer in two minutes. This settles almost every question at the boards.

Scoring — cancellation

In the hole = 3, on the board = 1, on the ground = 0. Only one team scores a round: higher total minus lower, and you score the difference.

Winning

First to 21 or more at the end of a full round wins. Going over is fine — 23 wins. No "bust," no win-by-two. Check the score only after all 8 bags are thrown.

Throwing

Four bags each, alternating one at a time. Last team to score throws first next round. Step over the line, or hit the floor first, and the bag's dead.

Speed lever

Standard game is to 21. On a packed night with a long bracket, drop to 15 to clear games faster — decide before the round, announce it, keep it consistent.

If it goes sideways

Already planned for.

Every night has a curveball. Here's the move for each one, so nobody's improvising at the boards — the answer's already decided.

A thin crowd

Run it anyway — small and loud. Under 8? Play singles. A handful of teams still fills two boards and makes noise. Grab the photos, post the recap — a real night on the feed is what pulls the next crowd in.

Too many to fit (16+)

Good problem. Drop games to 15 to clear faster, start a waitlist for next week, or split into two flights. Twelve is the sweet spot on two boards; past sixteen, don't let the bench sit long.

An odd number

Get to an even 8+ so you can run doubles: the runner sits in or plays, or comp a newcomer's entry to round it out. Doubles is the better night — worth a free entry to unlock it.

A game's dragging

Call the 20-minute cap: highest score wins, or one sudden-death round. Announce the next two pairs out loud so a board never sits empty — dead board time is the enemy.

A scoring dispute

Point to the rules. Cancellation scoring, counted out loud before you clear the boards, settles almost all of them. If a single bag's truly 50/50, re-throw just that bag.

The app dies

The printed bracket and a pen live on the table every week for exactly this. Switch over and keep the night moving — nobody even notices.

A no-show after the draw

Before games start, just re-draw. Mid-bracket, that team forfeits the current game — or drop in a willing player from the bench if everyone's cool with it.

Not sure it landed

One good Monday is enough to build on. Every night captures a photo and a winner; every recap makes the next Thursday's post stronger. It compounds — you're never starting from zero twice.

The multiplier

Two things make next Monday bigger.

The night runs itself. These two habits are what turn one good Monday into a standing crowd.

Grab a few photos

Two or three every night — winners, a packed board, the 4-Bagger. It's the one thing no graphic can fake, and it's the whole recap.

Post the Tuesday recap

Winner and a photo, the next morning. Proof is the best recruiter — it makes Thursday's intro post land harder every single week.

Capture a contact at sign-up

A phone or email from each player builds a list. Text the carryover pot before the next Monday — the single best way to fill the room.
