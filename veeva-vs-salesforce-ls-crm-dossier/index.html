01

What stands out

~5 min

Before the observations: Veeva Vault CRM is the depth-and-vertical-integration platform. It is vertical SaaS purpose-built for pharma commercial since 2007 on its own Vault Platform substrate. Salesforce Life Sciences Cloud (LSC) is the open-ecosystem-and-platform-AI platform: a vertical product on the broader Salesforce platform, generally available October 2025, evolving rapidly. The observations below pull from across both substrates and find the patterns that recur. Each one states what the evidence shows, with two-sided reads kept two-sided rather than collapsed.

  • Feature depth vs roadmap velocity Feature depth today favors Veeva; roadmap velocity favors Salesforce. Both are true. Across the seven canonical Life Sciences CRM capability areas, Veeva leads decisively today on call planning, MSL (Medical Science Liaison) workflows, sample management, MCCP (multi-channel cycle planning), and content / MLR (Medical, Legal, Regulatory) review *. Salesforce LSC is announced-closing the gap through 2026 with Medical Inquiry Management (GA), Sample Inventory Management (Oct 2026+), Events Management (Oct 2026+), and Cycle Plans in Engagement Planning *. AI is real on both sides, with divergent strategies. Veeva ships 8+ in-application agents across Vault CRM, PromoMats, Safety, and Quality, plus the new Falcon agentic-labor platform (early adopter Nov 2026) and the March 2026 Ostro acquisition (~$100M) marketed for "zero hallucination" conversational AI *. Salesforce Agentforce LS is broader and platform-level, and Salesforce currently leads on MCP (Model Context Protocol) openness. Agentforce LS already ships as headless services running inside Anthropic Claude per Salesforce SVP/GM Joe Ferraro *. Veeva CEO Peter Gassner committed Vault to becoming an MCP server during 2026 (September 2025 LinkedIn) but the native Vault MCP server has not yet shipped *. Accuracy is contested ground. Salesforce's own Customer Zero May 2026 reported 92% accuracy, meaning 8% inaccuracy, material in pharma compliance *; practitioner skepticism is documented across Reddit and analyst posts *; no public head-to-head benchmark exists. Sunshine reporting is a draw *. T1
  • Architecture philosophy Architecture philosophy split. Salesforce frames "open ecosystem"; Veeva frames "vertical integration." Both are accurate self-descriptions. Salesforce runs a single shared multitenant database, with sObjects virtual not physical. Apex enforces hard per-transaction limits: 100 SOQL (Salesforce Object Query Language) queries, 150 DML (Data Manipulation Language) statements, and a 10-second synchronous CPU cap. The development paradigm is Lightning Web Components, Flow, and Apex *. Veeva Vault is a managed application platform with controlled APIs and per-Vault instance isolation. Configuration runs through MDL (Metadata Definition Language), the Java SDK runs as a service account, X-Pages handles UI (there is no equivalent of Lightning Web Components), and the Vault Object Framework supports roughly 500 custom objects with 500 fields each *. Both are building agentic AI in 2025-2026. Each is bolted to its own native substrate *. Salesforce explicitly frames its strategy as "open ecosystem" contrasting Veeva *, with agent-to-agent collaboration as its architectural bet against Veeva's vertical integration *. T1
  • Customer-base shape Customer-base divergence is real and bifurcated, with marquee migrations both directions. Veeva counts 10 of top-20 biopharma committed at FY26 close *; a separate 247WallSt tracker puts top-20 commitments at 14 in Q3 FY26 *. Most likely a metric-definition difference (Veeva's "committed" vs the tracker's broader read), flagged as such. Salesforce LSC: 140+ organizations by May 2026, doubled in nine months from 70+ in Aug 2025 *. Marquee defections both ways. Dual-vendor patterns emerging as their own cohort, not exotic *. T1
  • TCO transparency Pricing-transparency asymmetry. Salesforce publishes list pricing across four SKUs; Veeva publishes zero. The asymmetry itself is the finding. Salesforce LSC has list pricing on four published SKUs, Premier Success Plan signature support pricing, and an announced 6% average list-price increase on Aug 1, 2025 *. Veeva publishes zero list pricing for any Vault product, including Vault CRM *. The procurement signal that does exist: the Vendr median for Veeva Systems contracts is $211,872/year *. Vault CRM also runs geography-tiered licensing in three tiers *. This is observation, not critique. The asymmetry is a fact about how each vendor approaches procurement disclosure. T1
  • Transition dates The transition arc has two dates, not one. They are different things announced in different documents. September 2030 is the contractual five-year wind-down clause embedded in the original Veeva-Salesforce agreement *. December 2029 is Veeva's separately-announced product-support extension on legacy Veeva-on-Salesforce CRM *. They were announced in two different documents. The product-support extension is Veeva signaling a longer legacy runway to give migration time. The contractual wind-down is the hard customer-facing forcing function. Conflating them is the obvious failure mode. T1
  • Catch · F8.17 No public Forrester Wave or Gartner Magic Quadrant for Life Sciences CRM exists in 2024 or 2025. KLAS LS-CRM ranking also does not appear to exist publicly. Generic "industry analysts say..." framings die at this fact *. The defensible analyst-layer floor: Everest Group's "wash" position on the separation *, plus RBC's Rishi Jaluria as the most consistent sell-side voice on the transition across 2023-2026 *. This catch surfaces here because it constrains every analyst-layer claim downstream of it. T1

05

AI capabilities: what each platform actually ships, and what the reviews say

~6 min

AI is the most-watched battlefield between the two platforms in 2026 and the one most prone to vendor spin. This section breaks the picture into three parts: what Veeva has actually shipped, what Salesforce has actually shipped, and what the practitioner record (reviews, news, vendor admissions) says about how it is performing. MCP (Model Context Protocol) and GenAI (generative AI) are expanded on first use. Citations point to thread 9 findings (F9.x) where the evidence lives.

Block A: Veeva AI: what is shipped

Veeva has eight publicly-named application agents across its Vault products as of June 2026, with more on a phased roadmap. The Dec 2025 dossier framing of "3 named agents" is six months stale. The current roster *:

  • Vault CRM (live Dec 2025): Free Text Agent (detects and flags issues in call notes), Voice Agent (voice input plus transcription for call reports), Pre-call Agent (insights and recommended actions for call prep).
  • PromoMats (live Sept 2025): Quick Check Agent (automated MLR (Medical, Legal, Regulatory) pre-checks on promotional content), Content Agent (context-aware agentic AI for content creation and MLR review).
  • Vault Safety (26R1 release, Feb-Apr 2026): Narrative Agent (generates case narratives).
  • Vault Quality (April 2026 wave): Quality agents shipped per Veeva roadmap and confirmed in Clarkston Consulting and partner posts. Specific agent names not yet itemized in public release notes.
  • Vault AI Chat (live April 15, 2026) and Vault AI Tab (Aug 2026 preview): conversational layer across Vault applications.

Veeva Falcon, announced May 27, 2026. Veeva's "first offering in agentic labor," positioned a tier above the per-application agents. Falcon delivers standard agents for clinical, regulatory, and safety: trial master file document intake and QC, health authority correspondence, and safety case triage and intake. Early adopter program November 2026; broader availability later *. T1

Ostro acquisition, March 2026, approximately $100M. Veeva acquired Ostro for conversational AI explicitly marketed as "zero hallucination" for patient and HCP (Healthcare Professional) brand engagement. Answers are drawn from MLR-approved content only. This is a deliberate positioning move against probabilistic-agent risk in regulated environments *. T1

MCP commitment, not yet shipped. Veeva CEO Peter Gassner stated on LinkedIn September 4, 2025: "Vault next year will become an MCP server and that's what enables agents talking to agents. And that's going to enable a lot of things." As of June 2026 the native Vault MCP server has not shipped publicly. Third-party CData has a read-only Veeva MCP server wrapper available today *. T1

Veeva's posture across all of the above: standard agents run in-context inside Vault applications, with model layer fixed to Anthropic and Amazon models hosted on Amazon Bedrock. Customers can bring their own models on AWS Bedrock or Microsoft Azure AI Foundry for custom agents only. This is a deliberate single-model-layer architectural choice for compliance reliability *.

Block B: Salesforce Agentforce LS: what is shipped

GA (general availability) features today. Agentforce Conversation, Account Summarization, Next Best Customer, and Tableau Next Dashboards are live *. Salesforce's Q1 FY27 earnings disclosed 28.6 trillion AI tokens processed (up 152% quarter-over-quarter) and $1.2B Agentforce ARR. These are vendor-published metrics; no independent third-party validation exists for the isolation of $1.2B to Agentforce specifically vs broader Data Cloud *. T1

MCP shipped today: the load-bearing finding. Joe Ferraro (Salesforce SVP/GM Life Sciences) describes Salesforce's strategy as "popping the top off the platform, exposing all the APIs, and exposing all the data models and workflows as MCP tools." Ferraro cites a live customer: "I was with a customer yesterday who has made a significant investment in Anthropic, and they are going to use our MCP service for Agentforce Life Sciences to bring all of those compliant workflows directly inside Claude." Salesforce currently leads Veeva on MCP openness *. T2

IQVIA OCE substrate partnership (April 2024). Salesforce licensed IQVIA's Orchestrated Customer Engagement CRM software to accelerate Life Sciences Cloud development. This is the partnership substrate that put the pharma-specific workflows on the platform *. T1

Customer Zero May 2026: Salesforce's own admission. Salesforce's May 12, 2026 blog reported its own Agentforce deployment trending at 92% accuracy, 89% task completion, and 94% engagement. The honest read: 8% inaccuracy is material in pharma compliance contexts (sample disbursement, MLR-approved content delivery, PDMA-validated workflows, sunshine reporting). Salesforce attributes residual gaps to "customer data hygiene" rather than LLM (Large Language Model) capability *. T1

Architecture thesis: broader, horizontal, headless. Salesforce's Agentforce LS is platform-level: model choice across Anthropic and others, agent-builder breadth, and MCP-tool exposure that lets the same workflows run inside any AI assistant. Salesforce uses Agent Script (a deterministic overlay) to weave compliance checks, approved templates, and consent status into the probabilistic LLM layer: "The model can synthesize and surface, but the regulated action runs through a fixed set of tools backed by deterministic workflows and confined actions." This is Salesforce's structural answer to the compliance-in-regulated-industries question *. T1

The 77% failure-rate statistic

Three industry sources (Solutions4SF, Oliv.ai, Bluprintx) cite an approximately 77% B2B Agentforce implementation failure rate, with failure causes broken down as 32% data quality, 18% hidden TCO surprise, 12% scope creep, 8% hallucination per Solutions4SF. Methodology caveat: the primary source of the 77% number is not perfectly traceable; treat as a widely-repeated industry directional statistic rather than peer-reviewed finding. The 77% figure is also directionally consistent with Salesforce CMO Ariel Kelman's own public admission that "95% of generative AI pilots fail", which frames a broader industry reality, not a Salesforce-specific defect *. T2 T3

140-orgs Ferraro clarification

The 140+ Agentforce LS organizations cited by Salesforce in May 2026 are not 140 organizations replacing Veeva CRM. Per Ferraro: "The 140 represents customers across our entire portfolio, regardless of whether they are using it for patient engagement, clinical engagement, commercial engagement or medical engagement." The headline number is real; the apples-to-apples read against Vault CRM commitments is narrower *. T1

Hallucination concerns and the pharma-compliance frame

Hallucinations are documented across Agentforce deployments (Salesforce Ben, Astrea IT, Gearset's Mehmet Orun, LinkedIn practitioner posts). Salesforce's framing positions the cause as customer data hygiene, not LLM capability. In a regulated environment, 8% inaccuracy intersects directly with GxP, 21 CFR Part 11, sunshine reporting, and MLR review obligations. The structural concern is not dramatic, it is procedural: the regulated action needs a deterministic envelope around the probabilistic synthesis layer. Both vendors have arrived at this principle. Implementation philosophy is where they diverge *. T2

The honest boundary: no public head-to-head benchmark

No public head-to-head accuracy benchmark exists comparing Veeva AI Agents and Salesforce Agentforce LS in life sciences as of June 2026. Stating this is itself a finding. Anyone claiming "Veeva AI is X% more accurate" or "Agentforce is materially weaker" is making it up. The defensible read is the divergence in posture and shipped capability, not a head-to-head accuracy number *. T1

Closing read

AI is real on both platforms. The honest read in 2026: Veeva ships more named agents and has acquired hallucination-positioning intellectual property; Salesforce currently leads on MCP-style openness with live Anthropic Claude integration. Both face the broader industry reality that most GenAI pilots fail and that compliance-grade accuracy is still being engineered. No public head-to-head benchmark in life sciences exists, so direct accuracy comparisons remain speculation. Convergence on MCP architecture is likely by end-2026.

Transition arc: bounded facts vs open scenarios

~6 min

The transition arc is where the line between evidence and interpretation matters most. Some facts are bounded: dates, customer trajectories, announced product milestones. Some scenarios are open: adoption curves, dominant patterns, end-states. The two get visually separated below rather than blended into a single narrative.

The two-date distinction

▸ Two dates, two documents

September 2030: the contractual 5-year wind-down clause in the original Veeva-Salesforce agreement. Hard customer-facing forcing function for the platform decision *.

December 2029: Veeva's separately-announced product-support extension on legacy Veeva-on-Salesforce CRM *. Announced separately, in a different document, after the original agreement. It is the support-runway, not the contractual deadline.

Conflating them collapses two facts into one. The discipline is to keep both visible.

Vault CRM trajectory: clean across quarters

Vault CRM live-customer count across FY23 to FY26: 60, 80, 100+, 115, ~140 live customers *. The original Vault CRM migration announcement was embedded in Veeva's Q3 FY2023 earnings call on Dec 1, 2022, not a standalone press release *. As of FY26 close (Jan 31, 2026): 125+ live customers, 10 of top-20 biopharma committed per Veeva's own framing *. T1
